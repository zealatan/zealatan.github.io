mmWave MIMO

28 GHz mmWave MU-MIMO Testbed

A programmable multi-user MIMO prototype operated over the air at 28 GHz, with real-time beam tracking — built and demonstrated at RWTH Aachen's Chair for Distributed Signal Processing, and published as a first-author paper at IEEE WCNC 2025.

IEEE Paper → Demo Video →

1Engineering Challenge

Multi-user MIMO and beam tracking at mmWave frequencies are usually studied in simulation because building a real over-the-air (OTA) testbed is hard: synchronization, beam management, and multi-user separation all have to work against real propagation, real hardware impairments, and real timing — not an idealized channel model. The goal of this project was to build a programmable 28 GHz testbed that could actually demonstrate multi-user operation live, not just predict it.

2System Overview & Hardware Platform

The testbed is an SDR-based mmWave platform assembled at the RWTH Aachen Chair for Distributed Signal Processing. It pairs baseband/SDR processing units with RF up/down-conversion stages and phased-array front-ends, and was demonstrated live in a two-user MIMO scenario with independent per-user signal chains and real-time beam control.

3My Contribution

I am the first author of the associated publication and personally built the testbed — from architecture and hardware integration through to the live OTA demonstration — as part of my research assistant work at the RWTH Aachen Chair for Distributed Signal Processing.

4Experimental Setup & Validation

The testbed was validated with a live two-user MIMO scenario, operated over the air rather than purely in simulation. The system and demonstration were presented at IEEE WCNC 2025 in Milan, Italy, alongside the first-author paper describing the design.
