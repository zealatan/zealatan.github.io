1. Common signal model QAM mapping · noise · detection · metrics

common

Normalized square QAM with Gray mapping

Modulation order \(M\in\{4,16,64,256\}\), \(L=\sqrt{M}\) levels per axis. Per-axis levels and Gray code:

$$ v_i = 2i-(L-1),\quad i=0,\dots,L-1, \qquad g(i)= i \oplus (i\!\gg\!1) $$

A normalization constant forces unit average symbol energy \(E_s=1\):

$$ c=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\cdot\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=0}^{L-1}v_i^2}},\qquad s = c\,(v_I + j\,v_Q) $$
QPSK/16QAM/64QAM/256QAM buttons · buildAxis() normFactor() grayEncode()
common

AWGN — Box-Muller Gaussian

$$ u,v\sim\mathcal U(0,1),\qquad z=\sqrt{-2\ln u}\,\cos(2\pi v)\ \sim\ \mathcal N(0,1) $$

SNR is converted to linear scale and the complex noise variance split equally between I and Q:

$$ \gamma=10^{\mathrm{SNR_{dB}}/10},\qquad n = \sigma\,(z_1+j z_2),\quad \sigma=\sqrt{\tfrac{\sigma_n^2}{2}} $$
SNR slider · randn() (the sine component is recycled via a spare variable)
②

Assembling the received noise variance — the simulator's central equation

The total noise variance visible on the equalized constellation is the sum of four terms:

$$ \sigma_n^2 \;=\; \underbrace{\ell_{est}\cdot\frac{P_t}{\gamma}}_{\text{AWGN}\times\text{est.\ loss}} \;+\; \underbrace{F_{sel}}_{\text{selectivity floor}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{\pi^2}{3}\Big(\frac{\Delta f_{res}}{\Delta f_{sc}}\Big)^{2}}_{\text{CFO ICI}} \;+\; \underbrace{(3.6\times10^{-4}\,\delta_{sfo})^2}_{\text{SFO residual}} $$

with pilot-overhead power \(P_t=0.97\), subcarrier spacing \(\Delta f_{sc}=20\,\mathrm{MHz}/256=78.125\,\mathrm{kHz}\), and channel-estimation loss

$$ \ell_{est}=1+(\,\ell_P-1\,)\cdot\mu_{noise},\qquad \ell_P=1.6\ \text{(PILOT\_LOSS)} $$

\(\mu_{noise}\) and \(F_{sel}\) are functions of the estimation options, defined in §6. With Perfect Sync ON, the CFO/SFO/PN residual terms are zero.

Perfect Sync toggle, PN/CFO/SFO sliders · the nVar assembly in simulate()
common

Hard decision · BER · EVM · blind SNR

$$ \hat i=\mathrm{clip}\Big(\mathrm{round}\big(\tfrac{y/c+(L-1)}{2}\big),\,0,\,L-1\Big), \qquad \mathrm{BER}=\frac{\#\{\text{Gray-bit mismatches}\}}{2\log_2\!L\cdot N} $$
$$ \mathrm{EVM}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_k |y_k-s_k|^2}{\sum_k |s_k|^2}},\qquad \widehat{\mathrm{SNR}}_{blind}=10\log_{10}\frac{1}{\mathrm{EVM}^2} $$
bottom status line BER · EVM · blind · sliceIdx(), tallies in simulate()

2. RF / sync impairment equations toggles shared by both tabs

common

CFO → ICI (inter-carrier interference)

A frequency offset \(\Delta f\) breaks subcarrier orthogonality; the equivalent noise (small-offset approximation):

$$ \sigma^2_{ICI}\;\approx\;\frac{\pi^2}{3}\Big(\frac{\Delta f}{\Delta f_{sc}}\Big)^{2} $$
CFO slider · cfoICI (②), and in syncConst() (①)
common

Phase noise (PN) — random walk + CPE

Phase noise of linewidth \(\beta\) [Hz] accumulates as a per-symbol random walk:

$$ \phi_{t+1}=\phi_t+w_t,\quad w_t\sim\mathcal N\!\big(0,\ (0.18)^2\,\tfrac{2\pi\beta}{\Delta f_{sc}}\big) $$

With common-phase (CPE) tracking ON the common component of \(\phi\) is removed (§13); OFF, the whole constellation rotates by \(\phi_t\).

PN slider + CPE toggle · pnSigma, phiRes
common

IQ imbalance

Gain mismatch \(g=10^{1/20}\) (1 dB), phase mismatch \(\varphi=5^\circ\):

$$ y' \;=\; g\,(\sin\varphi\; x+\cos\varphi\; y) $$
IQ toggle · iqG, iqP — classified as an impairment the synchronizer does not correct
common

PA nonlinearity — Rapp model (p = 2)

Saturation level \(A_{sat}=10^{3/20}\) (3 dB backoff):

$$ G(|x|)=\Big(1+\big(\tfrac{|x|}{A_{sat}}\big)^{2p}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2p}},\quad p=2 \qquad x'\!=G(|x|)\,x $$
PA toggle · Asat — envelope compression also squashes the timing-correlation peak (§10)
common

DC offset · SFO

$$ x' = x + 0.02 \qquad\quad \sigma^2_{SFO}=(3.6\times10^{-4}\cdot \delta_{ppm})^2 $$

DC mostly hits the DC subcarrier (small effect on data); SFO appears as a phase ramp proportional to symbol index (§13), and its residual enters as a variance term.

DC toggle · SFO slider · dc, sfoRes/sfoVar

②

Physical channel synthesis — exponential-PDP Rayleigh/Rician

Tap gains are Rayleigh complex gains on an exponential power-delay profile; Rician adds a LOS component to the first tap, then the CIR is normalized to unit power:

$$ g_n = e^{-0.35 n}\,e^{j\theta_n},\ \theta_n\sim\mathcal U(0,2\pi) \qquad g_0 \mathrel{+}= 1.6\ \text{(Rician)} \qquad \sum_n |g_n|^2 = 1 $$

The frequency response is the DFT over \(N=128\) subcarriers, with Doppler slowly modulating the tap phases:

$$ H[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{L_{cir}-1} g_n\,e^{j\,0.6\sin(t f_d/700+1.3n)}\;e^{-j2\pi kn/N} $$
the true|H| curve in the |H| panel · genBaseTaps() trueH()
①

Multipath for the synchronizer + Rician K

The CIR the preamble passes through (tap spacing 3 samples) uses the same exponential PDP, with a LOS of K-factor \(K_{dB}\) added to the first tap:

$$ g_0 \mathrel{+}= \sqrt{10^{K_{dB}/10}},\qquad r[m]=\sum_t g_t\,p[m-3t]\,e^{j2\pi \Delta f m/F_s}+n[m] $$
taps slider + Rician K slider · syncTiming() — the cause of the first-path ambiguity in §10

4. Channel estimation LS · LMMSE · Linear/FFT/2D/CNN interpolation

②

LS pilot estimates

\(P\in\{8,16,32,64\}\) pilots are placed with spacing \(D=N/P\). On pilot subcarriers:

$$ \hat H_p[i] \;=\; \frac{Y[iD]}{X_p[iD]} \;=\; H[iD]+\frac{n_i}{X_p},\qquad \operatorname{Var}=\sigma_n^2 $$
pilots 8/16/32/64 · est LS · Hpr/Hpi in estimateChannel() (orange dots)
②

Linear interpolation

$$ \hat H[k]=\hat H_p[i]+\frac{k-k_i}{k_{i+1}-k_i}\big(\hat H_p[i\!+\!1]-\hat H_p[i]\big) $$

On a selective channel it cannot follow the curvature between pilots — an interpolation bias that does not vanish with SNR and becomes a BER floor (§6).

interp Linear · lerp()
②

FFT (DFT) interpolation — CIR recovery

Take the pilot response to the time domain with a \(P\)-point IDFT, keep only the CP span (16 taps), and go back with an \(N\)-point DFT:

$$ c[n]=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{m=0}^{P-1}\hat H_p[m]e^{j2\pi mn/P} \;\xrightarrow{\;n<\min(P,16)\;}\; \hat H[k]=\sum_{n} c[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N} $$

If the channel length fits inside the CP, the bias disappears and noise shrinks by \(\tfrac{L_{cir}}{P}\).

interp FFT · idftP() dftToN()
②

LMMSE — per-tap Wiener weighting

Same path as FFT interpolation, multiplied by a per-tap Wiener filter with an exponential-PDP prior:

$$ w_n=\frac{p_n}{p_n+\sigma_n^2/P},\qquad p_n=\frac{e^{-0.35n}}{\sum_m e^{-0.35m}} \qquad c[n]\leftarrow w_n\,c[n] $$

Suppressing the noise-dominated weak taps cuts estimation noise by a further ~28%.

est LMMSE · the LMMSE branch of estimateChannel()
②

2D interpolation — time-axis averaging

$$ \bar H_p[i]=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\hat H_p^{(t)}[i],\quad T\le 5 \;\;\to\;\; \text{linear interpolation} $$

Only valid at low Doppler (long coherence time) — on fast channels like EVA/ETU the time average itself becomes a bias.

interp 2D · 5-frame moving average over chHist
②

CNN interpolation — learned residual refinement (AI CE)

The linear-interpolated estimate is RMS-normalized into a 3-channel input \([\mathrm{Re}/\rho,\ \mathrm{Im}/\rho,\ \sigma_n]\); a circular-padded 1-D convolutional residual network outputs a correction:

$$ h=\mathrm{ReLU}(W_{head}*x),\quad h\leftarrow h+\mathrm{ReLU}(W_{b}*h)\ (\times2),\quad \hat H = \big(x_{1,2}+W_{tail}*h\big)\cdot\rho $$

12 channels, kernel 7, two residual blocks. Weights were trained per pilot count in py_ofdm_sim's cnn_export.py and exported to JSON — the forward pass genuinely runs in the page.

interp CNN · cnnInterp() cnnConv(), weights CNN_W (inlined in the HTML)
②

Estimate quality + CFO correction (before/after)

$$ \mathrm{MSE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}\big|\hat H[k]-H[k]\big|^2 $$

CFO correction ON: the common phase is tracked from the inter-frame pilot inner product and de-rotated:

$$ \theta \mathrel{+}= \arg\!\Big(\sum_i \hat H_p^{(t)}[i]\,\overline{\hat H_p^{(t-1)}[i]}\Big), \qquad \hat H \leftarrow \hat H\,e^{-j\theta} $$

The CFO rotation is removed, but the SFO ramp and phase-noise jitter remain — the same limitation as real per-symbol CPE tracking.

CFO corr (before/after) button · MSE readout · chThetaAcc

5. Equalization ZF vs MMSE

②

Per-subcarrier equalizers

$$ \hat s_{ZF}[k]=\frac{Y[k]}{\hat H[k]} \qquad \hat s_{MMSE}[k]=\frac{\hat H^*[k]}{|\hat H[k]|^2+\sigma_n^2}\,Y[k] $$

For per-subcarrier hard detection in OFDM, normalizing out the MMSE bias yields the same decision boundaries as ZF, so BER does not change — which is why the simulator does not let the eq choice move the BER. This conclusion is calibrated against the Python comparison (py_ofdm_sim/compare_eq.py).

eq ZF/MMSE buttons · see the EQ_OPTS comment

6. BER curve model Monte-Carlo + per-option noise & floors

②

Curve generation (scenario mode)

SNR grid \(0..38\,\mathrm{dB}\) in 2 dB steps; 30,000 Monte-Carlo symbols per grid point:

$$ \sigma_n^2(\gamma)=\ell_{est}\frac{P_t}{\gamma}+F_{sel},\qquad \mathrm{BER}=\widehat{\Pr}\{\text{Gray bit flip}\} $$
↻ recompute button · berPoint() computeBer()
②

Estimation options → noise multiplier \(\mu_{noise}\)

$$ \mu_{noise}=\underbrace{\sqrt{32/P}}_{\text{pilot count}}\cdot \underbrace{\kappa_{est}}_{\substack{LS:1\\ LMMSE:0.72}}\cdot \underbrace{\kappa_{interp}}_{\substack{CNN:0.80\ /\ 2D:0.82\\ FFT:0.92\ /\ Lin:1}} $$

The 0.82 for 2D applies only when Doppler < 70 Hz (1 on fast channels).

noiseMulFor()
②

Selectivity error floor \(F_{sel}\)

Methods that recover the CIR (FFT/2D/CNN/LMMSE) and plain Linear meet different fates:

$$ F_{sel}= \begin{cases} 0.02\,\mathrm{res}+\mathrm{res}\cdot\iota\cdot u\cdot 0.5, & \text{CIR recovered}\quad u=\max(0,\tfrac{12}{P}-\tfrac12)\\[4px] 0.9\,\mathrm{res}\cdot(32/P)^{1.4}, & \text{Linear interpolation-bias floor} \end{cases} $$

\(\iota\): CNN 0.5, 2D 0.6, FFT 0.8 (×0.8 if LMMSE). \(u\) is an undersampling term that kicks in when the pilots fail to sample the CIR (below P≈24). Calibrated so the default (LS·Linear·P=32) matches the original per-scenario res.

Compare mode: LS·Linear / LS·FFT / CNN / LMMSE curves · selectiveFloor() estFloorOpt() berPointOpt()

7. FEC Hamming(7,4) residual BER

②

Residual bit error rate after single-error correction

Code rate \(R=4/7\), correction capability \(t=1\). From a raw channel BER \(p\):

$$ p_{out}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=t+1}^{n} i\binom{n}{i} p^i (1-p)^{\,n-i},\qquad n=7 $$

Coding gain is measured as the SNR difference where the two curves cross BER \(=10^{-2}\) (log-linear interpolation, snrAtBer()): AWGN ≈ 3.0 dB, Rician ≈ 7.5 dB.

+FEC button (dashed curves) · fecResidualBer() showCodingGain()

8. Image transmission link quality made visible

common

BER → pixel bit flips

The TX image (112×112, RGB) is treated as a bitstream; bits are flipped at random at the link's current effective BER (\(p_{out}\) when FEC is ON):

$$ N_{flip}=\mathrm{round}\big(112\cdot112\cdot3\cdot8\cdot \mathrm{BER}_{eff}\big), \qquad b_{idx}\leftarrow b_{idx}\oplus 2^{u},\ u\sim\mathcal U\{0..7\} $$

9. Preambles m-sequence / Gold (BPSK, L = 127)

①

LFSR generation and cyclic shift

$$ x[i]=\big(x[i-3]+x[i-7]\big)\bmod 2,\qquad x_{init}=1110110 $$
$$ p[n]=2\,x\big[(n+43\,N_{id}^{(2)})\bmod 127\big]-1 \in\{\pm1\},\qquad N_{id}^{(2)}=2 $$

Selecting "gold" uses a second sequence from the same generator with shift index \((N_{id}^{(2)}+3)\bmod 7\). This is a direct port of the C reference's generate_m_sequence_bits() used in the real receiver.

10. Timing sync sliding cross-correlation

①

Matched-filter metric and the first-path ambiguity

$$ M[k]=\Big|\sum_{n=0}^{L-1} p[n]\; r[k+n]\Big|,\qquad \hat\tau=\arg\max_k M[k] $$

In multipath a later tap can beat the first one, so the \(\arg\max\) (red line) lands \(\Delta\) samples off the true first path (green dashed) — a classical matched-filter limitation (the Δ shown in the plot legend). The timing error adds decision noise:

$$ \sigma^2_{tim}= \begin{cases}(\epsilon_\tau/42)^2, & \epsilon_\tau\le 32\ \text{(inside CP: phase ramp only)}\\ (\epsilon_\tau/12)^2, & \epsilon_\tau>32\ \text{(outside CP: ISI)}\end{cases} $$
timing slider (true τ), Timing toggle, plot ① · syncTiming() firstErr

11. CFO estimation phase-slope LS fit

①

The phase ramp of z = rx·conj(ref)

Stripping the known preamble leaves a pure CFO rotation (\(|p[n]|=1\)):

$$ z[k]=r[k]\,\overline{p[k]}=e^{j2\pi\Delta f k/F_s}+n'[k] \quad\Rightarrow\quad \phi[k]=\mathrm{unwrap}\big(\arg z[k]\big) $$

A least-squares line through the unwrapped phase gives the frequency from its slope:

$$ \hat\beta=\frac{m\sum k\phi_k-\sum k\sum\phi_k}{m\sum k^2-(\sum k)^2}, \qquad \widehat{\Delta f}=\frac{\hat\beta F_s}{2\pi} $$

Coarse uses the first 32 samples only (large-offset capture); fine uses both sync blocks, \(2\times127\) samples (half the variance). With IQ imbalance on, \(\arg z\) is biased and the estimate skews.

Coarse CFO / Fine CFO toggles, plot ② (blue dots + red fit line) · syncCfoFit() slope()

13. Phase tracking CPE random walk · SFO ramp

①

Three curves over symbol index

$$ \mathrm{CPE}[i]=\sum_{m\le i} w_m\ \ (\text{random walk}),\qquad \mathrm{SFO}[i]=1.5\times10^{-3}\,\delta_{ppm}\cdot i\ \ (\text{ramp}) $$
$$ \mathrm{resid}[i]=(1-\mathbb 1_{CPE})\,\mathrm{CPE}[i]+(1-\mathbb 1_{SFO})\,\mathrm{SFO}[i] $$

CPE is a phase common to all subcarriers (estimable from pilots and removable); SFO is a slope proportional to subcarrier index — their signatures differ, which is exactly why they can be estimated separately. That separation is the point of this plot.
